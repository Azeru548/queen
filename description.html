history_edu Runway Genesis & Silhouette Anatomy

The Architecture of Radical Asymmetry (Paris, Autumn/Winter 2002)

Presented inside the Cour Carrée du Louvre during the landmark Autumn/Winter 2002 collection, this tailored coat embodies Yohji Yamamoto’s sublime tension between severe Savile Row precision and traditional Japanese ma (間) — the conscious utilization of negative space between garment and flesh.

Unlike conventional double-breasted block patterns, this silhouette features an elongated, off-center bias wrap anchored by a discreet inner harness strap. This structural webbing allows the coat to hang with effortless ease while suspended from the shoulder, preventing collar collapse during walking motion.

The fabric is the legendary Japanese high-twist worsted wool gabardine, custom spun by a historic Nishiwaki mill. When stationary, the jacket appears sculptural and solid; when activated by physical movement, the textile ripples with deep fluid chiaroscuro.

Archivist's Field Note

"AW 2002 represents the absolute technical peak of Yamamoto’s harness-suspended outerwear. Very few examples survived in this unworn state with original factory basting threads remaining on the rear vent."
